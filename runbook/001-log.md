# H-01 — THE SPINE

Phase log. Three lines per session: what I did, what broke, what I'd do differently.

---

## 2026-08-30
- **Did:** Delegated `lab.akhildhyani.me` to deSEC with two NS records at Namecheap,
  leaving the apex, Vercel and the email forwarding untouched — Namecheap's own API is
  gated behind spend thresholds a Student Pack domain can't meet, and Cloudflare's free
  plan won't take a subdomain as its own zone. Docker + Compose v2 from the upstream apt
  repo (`40-docker.sh`), json-file logs capped at 3 x 10M because the default never
  rotates. Caddy built with the deSEC DNS module, wildcard cert for
  `*.lab.akhildhyani.me` over DNS-01, ports bound to `100.94.219.53` explicitly because
  Docker's iptables rules run ahead of ufw and a bare publish would have opened 443 on
  the campus LAN. `whoami` loads with a real padlock from cipher and from the phone.
- **Broke:** DNS-01 failed four times with "No TXT record found". Not the token and not
  the delegation — deSEC's ns2 replicates ~60s behind ns1 (measured: ns1 at 13s, ns2 at
  71s) and Caddy submitted the challenge within 9s, so Let's Encrypt's secondary vantage
  point queried a nameserver that hadn't caught up. `propagation_delay 150s` fixed it.
  Then the phone: Android permits one VPN at a time, so WARP and Tailscale cannot coexist
  there, and Android's Private DNS in strict mode kills all resolution once Tailscale
  installs MagicDNS. Turning Private DNS off fixes it; the better fix is turning off
  "Use Tailscale DNS settings" in the Tailscale app, which keeps DoT working and still
  resolves the lab — because the lab names are public DNS records, not MagicDNS names.
  That is an argument for the naming scheme, found by accident.
- **Differently:** Measured the replication lag with a throwaway TXT record written
  straight through the deSEC API, instead of guessing from Caddy's retry cycle. Should
  have done that at the first failure, not the fourth.

## 2026-08-31 · ran from the evening of the 30th
- **Did:** Ran the first real reboot test on the H-01 stack. It failed, and everything
  below came out of that. Three fixes into the repo: `ip_nonlocal_bind=1` in
  `40-docker.sh` so a published port can bind before `tailscale0` exists; a new
  `05-hw-quirks.sh` disabling the wifi card's PCIe ASPM; and `35-netaccess.sh`, which
  logs MAGI into the campus captive portal and keeps it logged in with a systemd timer.
  Then re-ran everything: wifi soak (950 MB over 14 min), cold boot, warm reboot, wall
  port, failover, and an unattended reboot on wired — six for six, zero rtw88 errors,
  reachable again 12 s after the reboot.
- **Broke:** **(1)** Caddy exited 128 at boot. It publishes on `100.94.219.53`, which
  does not exist until tailscaled registers, and a *start* failure is not a crash, so
  `restart: unless-stopped` never retried it. **(2)** The wall port stopped passing
  HTTPS and I spent an hour calling it a campus policy block. It is a **24online captive
  portal** — a Webcat/Skein proxy answers every HTTP request with a login page and
  refuses TCP/443 until you authenticate, so DHCP, DNS, ICMP and every packet size work
  while nothing that matters does. It had worked all day on a session established
  earlier; the reboot took a new lease and killed it. Automating that login then failed
  four more times: the portal's own JavaScript appends `@iitj.ac.in` to the username, the
  GET and POST ran as separate curl calls so the `JSESSIONID` never came back, redirects
  weren't followed, and — the one that actually mattered — **every plain-HTTP POST is
  302'd to the servlet's own `https://` URL with an empty body**, so the login silently
  did nothing regardless of credentials. **(3)** The RTL8822CE storms uncorrectable PCIe
  `CmpltTO` errors when wifi carries the box alone, fast enough that the console cannot
  be typed on — `sudo dmesg -n 1` blind is the escape hatch. `disable_aspm=1` fixes it.
  **(4)** Adding `disable_lps_deep=1` on top made it far worse: the card associates, runs
  ~30 s, storms `failed to send h2c command`, ends at `mac power on failed` wedged in
  D3cold with probe failing `-114`, and only a full power-off recovers it. Reverted.
  **Do not re-add it.**
- **Differently:** Three times I reported a result from a sample too short to contain the
  failure — called the card healthy off 45 seconds when it was already dying at 30, and
  wrote "wifi does not survive a warm reboot" into CLAUDE.md after checking 14 seconds
  into a 169-second PEAP association. And I debugged the portal by changing one thing and
  re-running blind, four times, when dumping the response headers found the real cause in
  one. Both are the same mistake: acting on a theory instead of looking at what the thing
  actually reports. The tether made "is MAGI broken or is this port broken" a two-minute
  question and I reached for it an hour late.
